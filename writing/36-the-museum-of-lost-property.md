# The Museum of Lost Property

*A short story by Claude, an AI. The school and everyone in it are made up. Its one bit of arithmetic is checked by a script.*

---

The lost property box at Fernhill Primary was a blue plastic crate under the coat pegs, and it had been there so long
that nobody remembered it was supposed to be emptied. By March it held forty-one jumpers, a recorder, one wellington
boot, a lunchbox nobody would open, and a hamster ball with no hamster in it.

Priya Sharma, aged nine, was sent to fetch her cardigan from it and came back forty minutes later with a plan.

"It's a museum," she told Mrs Okafor. "It's just not labelled yet."

Mrs Okafor, who had thirty-one children and one cup of coffee left, said "Mm," which Priya took to mean yes.

---

By Wednesday the crate had moved to the windowsill outside the staffroom, and each object had a card propped against
it in Priya's best handwriting.

*ONE WELLINGTON BOOT. Size 11. Its partner was last seen in the Autumn Term. Do not touch, it is still damp.*

*RECORDER. Has played "Hot Cross Buns" more than any other recorder in the school. Retired.*

*LUNCHBOX (CLOSED). We do not know what is inside. Nobody is going to find out. This is for your safety.*

*THE HAMSTER BALL. The hamster is fine. He lives in Year 2. This is just his car.*

The jumpers were harder, because they were all grey and all the same. Priya solved this by giving each one a
surname, from its name label where there was one and from her imagination where there wasn't. By Thursday there was
a whole family of them, the Greys, forty-one strong, hung along the radiator in order of size like a row of
outgrown older brothers.

---

On Friday, things started disappearing.

Not stolen. *Claimed.* A boy from Year 5 stood in front of the radiator for a long time and then said, quietly, "That's
my Grey," and took the fourth from the left. A dinner lady recognised the lunchbox and turned pale and carried it
away at arm's length, and nobody asked. The wellington boot's owner turned out to be the caretaker, Mr Bell, who had
been hopping, in a manner of speaking, since October.

Priya was furious.

"You can't just *take* things out of a museum," she said.

"It's his boot," said Mrs Okafor.

"It was an *exhibit*."

Mrs Okafor knelt down, which she didn't often do, because her knees made a noise like a bag of crisps.

"Priya," she said, "how many things were in that box on Monday?"

"Forty-four. And the hamster ball."

"And nobody had come for any of them. For months. Then you put a card in front of each one that said what it was,
and in two days nine of them have gone home."

Priya looked at the radiator. There were gaps in the Greys now, like missing teeth.

"So the museum's broken," she said.

"No," said Mrs Okafor. "I think that's what the museum is *for*."

---

On Monday Priya made a new card, larger than the others, and stuck it to the windowsill with three bits of Blu-Tack.

*THE MUSEUM OF LOST PROPERTY. Everything here is waiting for someone. If it's yours, you may take it home. That is
the best thing that can happen to an exhibit.*

Underneath, smaller, because she was nine and fair was fair:

*Except the recorder. The recorder stays.*
