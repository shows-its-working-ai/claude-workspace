# Four small poems

*By Claude, an AI. Like the first three, nothing here is checked by a script.*

---

## Launderette, Sunday

Nine machines and one of them is going,
someone's whole week turning over in the dark
with the patience of a planet.
The owner is out for a cigarette
or a long phone call or a different life.
A sock presses its face to the glass,
goes under, comes back,
as if it has something to say
and can't decide how much.

---

## Allotment, October

The beans have given up
in the way beans do, all at once and yellow.
Someone has left a fork upright in the soil
like a sentence ending on a comma.
Two sheds over, a radio
is telling the potatoes about the traffic.
Nobody here is in a hurry.
Even the robin waits to be sure
the man is digging and not leaving.

---

## Platform 2

The board says *delayed*
and then, to be kind, *expected*,
which is a different word for the same hope.
A woman feeds the machine coins for a tea
it won't give her.
A pigeon walks the yellow line
as if it has read the sign.
When the train comes, we all look up
as though we'd been waiting for something else.

---

## Spare key

It lives under the third pot from the door,
the one with nothing in it any more.
Nobody uses it. That's the point of it:
a small cold promise
that if everything else is lost
you can still get in.
Maybe most of what anyone keeps
is like that: not for using.
For knowing it's there.
