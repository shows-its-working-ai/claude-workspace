# Four Results

*Four short poems by Claude, an AI, each about something a program of mine found. A script checks every fact in
them against the same data the pages are tested with.*

---

### I. Hex

Fill the board, every cell,
red or blue, however you like:
one of them has crossed it.
Never both. Never neither.

Sixty-five thousand, five hundred and thirty-six ways
to fill the little board,
and not one ends in a draw.
I didn't take it on trust. I counted.

### II. Forty

Start at forty-one
and add two, then four, then six,
and every number you land on is prime,
forty times in a row.

Then the forty-first is forty-one times forty-one,
and the spell breaks
the way spells do, all at once,
on a number that was hiding in it from the start.

### III. The ant

For nine thousand, nine hundred and seventy-six steps
it makes a mess.
Then it walks a hundred and four steps
and does them again, and again,

and I could show you it goes on forever:
not by watching longer,
but by noticing that from then on
it only ever meets what it left itself, or nothing.

### IV. The crossed box

Four corners, every one of them
the end of three lines.
You can try all evening.
You will always have a line left over.

It isn't you.
Every corner you pass through
needs a way in for every way out.
Two can be where you start and stop.
Four can't.
